The 5 one-day reports are also counted where the desk files them, under their tenant's cause.

  • parser: the shared parser reads every date month first, and every tenant on file writes the day first. The date comes out with day and month swapped.
  • hint: booking-v3’s form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it says, and the form then reads the date day first. Fixing the parser does not touch this.
  • one-day: the date moves by one day, as with a client in Lisbon getting Tuesday and a client in Toronto getting Monday. A month/day swap cannot do that. The desk had already read these reports this way (desk-notes.json) but filed them under the parser bug by tenant. Probably a booking sent without its time zone; an engineer has to confirm.

How the reports were placed

  • One report, one count. The export pages overlap, so some reports appear twice. Counted once by id (uniqueById).
  • By tenant, not by intake tag. Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser locale. The cause depends on the tenant’s locale and booking form (causeOf in causes.ts). The two disagree in both directions, so the tag is not a count.
  • One-day moves are their own problem, because no swap explains them. The desk files them under their tenant’s cause and footnotes them; this page counts them there too, and again on their own.

Why one ticket hid three

Triage merges on subject line alone (decision 0001). Every one of these reporters wrote some version of “wrong day”, so every report landed in one ticket. The decision accepted that cost for reports with different subjects about one bug. It did not cover the reverse: one subject about different bugs. That is this ticket.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can be many bugs with one symptom. Count by cause before you mark it known.
  • Group reports by the tenant’s locale and form, not by subject or intake tag.
  • Decision 0001 and the triage page have not been re-read since 2024. Their rule produced this ticket.
